\chapter{Experiments}

\subsection{Roles of the models in this thesis}
\label{subsec:model_roles}

The probabilistic models introduced above play deliberately different roles.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi} provides a fundamental homogeneous random graph model, a classical baseline, and the canonical example of a constant graphon.

    \item \textbf{Stochastic Block Models} provide community-based random graph distributions, classical baselines, and the canonical connection to block-structured graphons.

    \item \textbf{Degree-Corrected SBM} provides a baseline extending community structure with heterogeneous expected degrees.

    \item \textbf{Configuration Model} provides a degree-based baseline that controls degree information while randomizing much of the remaining structure.

    \item \textbf{Expected-degree graphs} provide a synthetic case useful for studying heterogeneous degree-driven probability patterns and smoother or rank-structured graphon-like representations.

    \item \textbf{Barab\'asi--Albert graphs} provide a synthetic stress case emphasizing preferential attachment and heterogeneous degree behavior.

    \item \textbf{Watts--Strogatz graphs} provide a synthetic stress case emphasizing local clustering and small-world organization.
\end{itemize}

These roles reflect the dataset and baseline design of the OGAD project. Their exact dataset parameters, sample counts, configurations, and measured behavior belong to the Experiments chapter.
